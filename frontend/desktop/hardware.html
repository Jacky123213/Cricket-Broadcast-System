HARDWARE GUIDE

Small field cameras

Use a Raspberry Pi when you need a camera that participates fully in live DRS and synchronized replay. Use ESP32/Arduino-class camera boards only as lightweight production feeds until a transcoder is added.

Raspberry Pi · supported

  1. Use Raspberry Pi OS with Chromium and a CSI or USB UVC camera.
  2. Install the Studio local CA certificate.
  3. Open the camera URL in Chromium and grant camera permission.
  4. Select the physical camera from the Available camera list.
  5. Choose 720p / 30 FPS before trying higher settings.

A Pi 4 or newer is recommended for browser WebRTC and MediaRecorder. Keep Chromium visible and disable screen sleep during a match.

ESP32-CAM / Arduino · limited

Most Arduino-class boards publish MJPEG or individual JPEG frames. They do not implement this app’s WebRTC signaling, clock sync or overlapping replay recorder.

You can add an ESP32-CAM stream directly to OBS as a separate source, then place the scoreboard overlay above it. It will not appear as a replay angle in this version.

Why the difference matters

DRS replay requires a stable clock, encoded rolling clips and two-way signaling. A microcontroller is excellent for a small live camera, but a Raspberry Pi has the browser and encoding resources needed for the full workflow.

Network advice

Use 5 GHz Wi-Fi for phones and Pis where coverage allows. Give fixed field devices DHCP reservations, avoid guest/client-isolated networks, and test every angle for at least 70 seconds.
